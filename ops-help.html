A normal day

Open ops. Home tells you what happened and what is waiting: today's orders and takings, and four cards that only speak up when something needs you — items out of stock, empty categories, new customers, poor reviews.

  1. Check Orders for anything marked receipt — somebody has paid and is waiting on you.
  2. Check Support for the dot, or the card in the bottom-right corner.
  3. Pack what is paid, and move each order along its status as you go.

The ☰ button, top left, opens the menu. You can also swipe in from the left edge of the screen. It closes when you pick something.

The back button

Your phone's back button undoes the last thing that happened on screen, in order: it closes an open panel first, then the menu, then returns you to the section you came from.

When there is nothing left to go back to it asks — press back again to exit — so a stray thumb does not close the console while you are working. The warning lapses after a couple of seconds.

Home

The strip across the top is money: orders and takings for today, then for the last seven days, then how many orders are waiting to pack and send. That last number turns amber when it is not zero.

Cancelled orders are never counted as takings.

The four cards below are shortcuts — tap one to go to that section. A card turns amber when it is telling you about something rather than just counting.

Products

Adding an item

  1. Products → + New product.
  2. Fill in the name, description and price, and choose a category.
  3. Pick a colour, then add photos for that colour. Repeat for each colour you stock. Every colour gets its own Add tile — you do not have to set anything up first.
  4. Set stock and price per colour and size in the grid. A pairing you leave empty simply is not offered to shoppers.
  5. Save, then set it to published when you want it live.

A short video can be uploaded alongside photos. Whichever media sits first in order is what shoppers see first, but a video never plays on its own.

Units sold

The sold figure is yours to enter — it shows under the quantity selector as 🔥 1.2k+ sold. It is not counted automatically, so it says whatever you put there.

Size guides

An item shows its category's size guide unless you give it one of its own. Give the body table a Height and a Weight column and shoppers also get a "find my size" box.

Categories

A category can sit under another one — that is how Collection and Jewellery hold their drop-downs.

A category holding a drop-down holds no stock of its own. When you open one, the "Items in this category" and "Size guide" cards are hidden on purpose: its items live on its children. File a product under Watches, not under Jewellery, or nobody browsing the drop-down will find it.

Orders

Each row shows the order number, the customer's name and email, what they spent and where the order has got to. Tap View for the full order.

What the colours mean

  • pending — placed, not paid for yet
  • paid — money received, needs packing
  • packed — boxed, waiting for the rider
  • shipped — on its way
  • delivered — arrived
  • cancelled / refunded — closed

Changing a status notifies the customer, so move an order along as it actually happens rather than in a batch at the end of the day.

Cancelling or refunding an order puts its stock back automatically, and returns anything paid from a store balance.

The filter

Receipt to check at the top of the filter is not a status — it shows only orders where a customer has sent proof of payment and is waiting on you.

Getting paid

How customers pay is set in Settings → Payment. There are two modes.

Bank transfer (what the shop uses now)

  1. The customer sees your account details and their order number.
  2. They transfer, then upload the receipt on their order page.
  3. You get a notification, and the order shows a receipt mark.
  4. Open it, tap View receipt, and check it against your bank.
  5. Tap Mark payment received. The customer is told automatically.

Only mark it once you have seen the money. A receipt is a picture somebody sent you, not proof the transfer cleared. This is the one action in ops that cannot be undone by pressing it again.

Paystack (card, transfer and USSD)

When Paystack has activated the business and a live key is in place, switch Settings → Payment to Paystack. Orders then confirm themselves the moment the payment clears and there is nothing to check off.

Until then, leave it on bank transfer. While the shop is on Paystack's test keys, only staff accounts can start a card payment — a customer would meet a payment they cannot complete.

Store balance

Customers can hold money with the shop and spend it here. It is store credit: it buys goods and cannot be withdrawn as cash.

  1. The customer transfers to your account and tells you, on chat or with a receipt.
  2. Open Customers, find them, and tap Add money.
  3. Enter the amount and a note saying where it came from.
  4. They are notified straight away, and can spend it at checkout.

Take off is there for a mistake or a chargeback. Both actions ask you to confirm, and the note you write is the only record of why it happened — so write a real one.

The customer sees their balance in the shop's header and a full statement under Account → Store balance.

Customers

Everyone who has an account, what they have spent, and how many orders they have placed. Opening one shows their details, their store balance and its recent movements.

  • Block stops an account signing in. It keeps everything.
  • Delete account removes the person. Their orders remain, because the shop's records are not theirs to erase.

Support

Every conversation with a customer, oldest message first. Realtime — a reply appears without refreshing.

  • A message arriving anywhere in ops shows a box across the top with who wrote and what they said. Open jumps straight into the conversation.
  • When that box goes, a small card stays in the bottom-right corner saying how many are waiting. It does not leave until they are answered, so a message cannot be quietly forgotten.
  • copy on any message copies its text. Copy all in the header copies the whole conversation with both names on it.
  • The paperclip attaches a file. The microphone next to it records a voice note — tap once to start, tap again to stop and it attaches itself. Two minutes maximum. Customers can send them too.
  • Mark closed ends a conversation; the customer keeps their history and can start a new one.

Reviews

Only customers who bought an item can review it. You cannot edit a review — you decide whether it is shown.

Hiding one also takes it out of the item's star average, so the rating always reflects what shoppers can actually read.

Settings

  • Payment — bank transfer or Paystack, and your account details.
  • Delivery — a flat fee, and the spend above which it is free.
  • Store — name, currency, hero heading, contact details, socials.
  • Instructions shown at checkout — the line under the payment section. Say to use the order number as the transfer reference; it is what makes matching a receipt to an order quick.

Press Save settings — nothing here saves on its own.

Alerts on your phone

Tap 🔔 Turn on alerts in the ops top bar and allow the prompt. You then get a notification for:

  • a new order, with its number and total
  • a customer writing in, with the first line of what they said
  • a receipt being uploaded

Alerts go to whichever account is signed in, so turn them on while signed in as staff. If the button says Alerts blocked, allow notifications for the app in your phone's settings, then reload.

If they do not pop up

Alerts should slide over whatever is on screen. If one only appears in the notification bar with a sound, check Settings → Apps → Rex-Giddoty Ops → Notifications:

  • the Orders and messages category should be set to Alerting or Urgent, not Silent
  • on Samsung, the notification style should be Alert rather than Brief — Brief shows a thin bar and never a full banner

This only applies to the installed app. Added to a home screen from Chrome instead, the notification belongs to Chrome and the shop cannot change how it behaves.

The app, and staying signed in

Ops installs to a home screen: ☰ → Install this app. On an iPhone that menu shows Add to Home Screen instead, which you do from the Share button.

Remember me on the sign-in page keeps your email so you only type a password. It never stores the password — that belongs to your browser's own password manager, which locks with the device.

Forgot password? emails a link that brings you back to ops. The link lasts an hour and works once.

Something not behaving? Reload the page twice. The first load fetches the new version of the console, the second runs it. If it still looks wrong, say so and it gets fixed.
